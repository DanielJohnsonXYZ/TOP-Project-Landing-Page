  • High-consideration ecommerce products

  • Brands competing in buyer guides and “best” queries
  • Teams that need judgement, not another automated score

Not a strong fit

  • Businesses looking for guaranteed rankings or mentions

  • Anyone expecting one prompt to represent all AI behaviour

Four failure modes

Separate problems that require context, not a universal score.

A brand may experience one, several or all four at once. Human review determines which pattern is commercially important for your market and buyers.

01

Absent

Your brand does not appear when customers ask relevant buying questions.

Impact: no consideration at the point of research.
02

Misunderstood

Answer engines associate you with the wrong category, use case or audience.

Impact: the right buyers receive the wrong reason to choose you.
03

Under-evidenced

Your strongest claims exist on your website but are not sufficiently corroborated elsewhere.

Impact: competitors look more credible even when your product is stronger.
04

Mentioned, but not chosen

You appear in the answer, but objections or weak positioning push the recommendation elsewhere.

Impact: you create awareness while competitors capture demand.

Frequently asked questions

Human judgement, methodology and uncertainty.

AI answers change and strategy is contextual. The audit makes the evidence, assumptions and analyst judgement visible rather than presenting one score as objective truth.

Is this an automated report?

No. We use technology to collect and organise outputs, but a human selects the questions, checks the evidence, compares conflicting signals and decides what matters for your customers and commercial priorities. You receive a reasoned diagnosis, not a dashboard export.

Why does the human layer matter?

One machine cannot account for every product quirk, customer attribute, buying pattern or internal priority. Two brands with the same visibility symptom may need completely different fixes. Experienced judgement connects the observed pattern to the actual decision journey.

Is the analysis subjective?

Partly, and we make that explicit. Platform outputs, cited sources and repeated patterns are evidence. The interpretation of commercial importance and priority requires judgement. The report separates observations from inferences so you can challenge or refine them.

How do you account for changing or inconsistent AI responses?

We use consistent prompts, record the date, market and platform, compare answers across systems and look for repeated brands, caveats and sources. Human review gives more weight to stable patterns than one unusual response.

Which platforms, markets and languages can you test?

The core audit can test ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Google. The standard scope supports English-language testing for the UK, US, Australia, Canada and relevant European markets. Other engines, countries and languages can be scoped separately.

How are the buying questions selected?

A human combines the product category, use cases, market terminology, competitor framing and common pre-purchase decisions. Client-supplied questions are useful, but we also assess whether they reflect how customers actually compare and choose.

Do you need analytics, website access or internal information?

No access is needed for the free mini-scan or core external audit. Analytics, search data, customer research and internal positioning can improve the interpretation, but they are optional and only requested with permission.

What exactly do I receive?

The mini-scan is a concise human-reviewed finding. The full audit includes the platform evidence, competitor visibility, cited sources, primary commercial gap, human interpretation, prioritised actions and a 45-minute readout.

Do you guarantee that AI tools will recommend us?

No. AI systems change and no responsible provider can guarantee a mention. The work improves the clarity, authority, corroboration and source signals that influence whether a brand is understood and selected.
